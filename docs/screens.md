# Study RPG — Screens, settings, platform, Glossary & Bestiary

Part of the game design reference; `game.md` is the index. Section numbers (§) are shared across all docs.

## 2. Screens **[Built]**

Four fundamental screens, each potentially containing sub-screens:
1. **Menu** (`ContentView`): landscape split. Left: the gilded title (`ScreenTitle`, tagline "Know your cards. Slay the dragon.") over the first two hero classes standing on small floating islands (the same `BattleFigureStand` as the fight stage, so their feet stand on the island top), in a warm pool of light. Right: a column of menu plaques (`MenuPlaqueButtonStyle` — the main action gold, the rest dark gilded panels), each with an icon medallion and one line of live info: **Continue** (only with a saved run: "Step N/M · ♥ HP · gold"), **New Run** (warns "Replaces your saved run" when one exists, or that a deck is needed), **Decks** (deck count · card count · overall % right), **History** (runs · wins), then one row of two half-width plaques, **Glossary** ("N/M found") and **Bestiary** ("N/M met") (§16.1). A small "Test Fight" chip sits under the plaques; Settings is a round gold-rimmed gear chip in the top-right corner (`ChromeIconButtonStyle`). Static.

**No navigation bars:** no screen shows a top bar or title — only the bar's buttons float over the scenery (`themedNavigationBar()`: hidden bar background, title removed, always inline and forced present even when empty, as on the main menu, so the top area has the same height on every screen and pushed screens don't shift down after the transition).
2. **Overworld (Map)** (`RunMapView`): the run's branching node map, scrolling left→right, with the run HUD on top (§9).
3. **Fight** (`FightView`): combat. Heroes on the left, 1–3 enemies on the right (§7).
4. **Special**: run setup (`RunSetupView`), shop (`ShopView`), campfire (`CampfireView`), event (`EventView`), skill reward (`SkillRewardView`, §5) and the run recap (`RunEndView`). Shop, campfire and event share the run HUD (`RunHUD`) at the top.

**Settings** (`SettingsView`, `GameSettings` in UserDefaults — presentation only, never balance) are **reachable from every screen**: the menu's corner chip, a gear in the toolbar of every pushed screen (`themedNavigationBar()` adds `SettingsGearButton`; the menu and the character info sheet opt out), the run HUD (map, shop, campfire, event), the fight's top bar (and over its victory/defeat screen), the skill offer and the run recap. Fast mode also toggles from the fight's top bar (hare/tortoise). Laid out for landscape: a gilded "Settings" title, then two parchment cards side by side — *Combat & Learning* and *Sound & Feel* — and a *Language* row under them, where every row is a colored icon medallion, a title, a one-line explanation and a gold toggle; a round close chip sits top-right. Scrolls only at very large text sizes.
- **Fast mode** (off by default): halves every combat pause, spins the dice faster, and throws an enemy's attack on both heroes as one batch of dice (§7.6, §8). Normal mode is unchanged.
- **Pause after a wrong answer** (on by default): the quiz waits for a tap after a wrong answer (§4.1).
- **Pause after a right answer** (off by default): the quiz also waits for a tap after a right answer; off = it moves on immediately (§4.1).
- **Sound effects** (on by default) and an **effects volume** slider (80% by default; letting go plays a sample coin). See §19.
- **Haptics** (on by default).
- **Language** (System by default; a row under the two cards: System / English / Deutsch, each language named in itself — `Localization` in `Services/Localization.swift`, UserDefaults `language`). Switches every screen at once, mid-run included; combat log lines already written stay in the language they were written in. A chosen language is also written to `AppleLanguages`, so system-provided text (photo picker, standard menu items) follows from the next launch; System removes it. See §13 "Localization".

## 15. Platform **[Built]**

- iPhone and iPad, landscape only. iPad shows the game's 16:9 canvas centered in a candlelit tabletop frame (`WidescreenCanvas`, §18).
- **Mac:** a native macOS app from the same target (not Mac Catalyst, not "Designed for iPad"). Everything that differs between UIKit and AppKit goes through `Views/Platform.swift` (`PlatformImage`/`PlatformColor`/`PlatformFont`, `PlatformGraphics.image(size:)` with a top-left origin on both, `Image(platformImage:)`, `hiddenNavigationBar()`, small iOS-only modifiers); other code uses `#if os(iOS)` only where a feature doesn't exist on Mac. Differences on Mac:
  - The window keeps its **standard title bar** (user decision 2026-10-26, an exception to §2's "no navigation bars"); a pushed screen's buttons (back, settings gear) sit in the window toolbar. Map and fight hide their toolbar items and use their own chrome. The app is always in dark appearance and the toolbar background is hidden, so the bar shows the night background instead of a bright system bar. Content never extends under the title bar on Mac (`ignoresSafeAreaOnIOS`), so the menu's gear isn't cut off.
  - The world map pans by click-and-drag as well as the trackpad (`DragToScroll` in `RunMapView`).
  - No camera in the card image menu (photo library only), no haptics, no system text size (`Font.readable` is fixed).
  - **Window** (`MyApp`, `WidescreenCanvas`/`CanvasFit` in `Views/StageFrame.swift`): freely resizable, minimum 960×540, default 1280×720, full screen allowed. The 16:9 canvas lays out normally up to `CanvasFit.maxLayout` (1366×768 pt, the iPad Pro canvas) and is scaled up evenly beyond that, so layouts never meet sizes iPhone/iPad don't have. Scaling enlarges the finished picture, so a scaled canvas is slightly soft, most visibly on non-Retina displays; the user accepted that (2026-10-26) over drawing at real size (sharp, but everything stays small on a big screen). Sharp *and* filling would need every screen to size itself from the canvas — a possible later rework. Extra space shows the tabletop: above and below in a tall window (as on iPad), left and right only in a window wider than iPhone landscape (19.5:9, `CanvasFit.widestFillAspect`); anything in between fills the window, as on iPhone. Inside a scaled canvas `displayScale` is raised by the scale, so rasterized views (map terrain, drawing groups) stay sharp. iPhone and iPad are unchanged (no side borders, no scaling).
  - **Sheets** (settings, party, character info, card editor) get `.gameSheetFrame()`: on Mac 90% of the canvas (`canvasSize` in the environment), at least 720×480 and at most 1100×800, or a content size the sheet asks for (Settings: 880×470); always within the canvas. iOS keeps the system size. Mac sheets can't be swiped away, so each has a close button; the card editor gets a Cancel (Esc) next to Save.
  - **Lists:** reordering decks and cards is a plain drag. Deleting is in the right-click menu — Delete Deck (with the usual confirmation) and, Mac only, Delete Card; there's no Edit button.
  - **Keys** (`macKeyboardShortcut`, Mac only): 1–4 pick a flashcard option and 5 picks "None of these" — on Mac the option badges show these numbers instead of A–E; Return presses Continue after an answer; Esc closes a sheet (its close button, the card editor's Cancel) or goes back from a pushed screen (`themedNavigationBar`, only on presented screens). Esc never leaves a shop or fight.
  - **Menu bar** (`Views/GameMenuCommands.swift`): a "Game" menu — New Run (⌘N), Continue, Decks — that only works on the main menu with no run open (`GameMenuRouter`, kept current by `ContentView`); File › New Window is removed (one game window). Settings… (⌘,) in the app menu opens `SettingsView` in its own window; the in-game gears stay (§2). The Haptics setting is hidden.
  - **Saved data:** the same files as on iOS (§3, §4), in Application Support inside the app's sandbox container (`~/Library/Containers/<bundle id>/Data/Library/Application Support/`). Writers create the folder if it's missing (`UserDataLocation.existingDirectory`). Deck import uses the system open panel (`.fileImporter`, user-selected files read-only); card photos come from the photo library (`PhotosPicker`), no camera.
  - **Hover:** every button style (`QuestButtonStyle`, menu plaques, `ChromeIconButtonStyle`, and `.gamePlain` — `.plain` with hover on Mac, exactly `.plain` on iOS), the selectable fight sprites and the relic tray brighten slightly under the pointer and show the pointing hand (`hoverHighlight()`).
- **Open items before release** (project settings Xcode's tool can't change — set them in the target's General/Build Settings tabs): the app icon set name must be `AppIcon` (General → App Icons) or no icon ships; `UIRequiresFullScreen` should be iPhone/iPad-only (macOS warns it's deprecated). Done 2026-10-26: outgoing network connections off (the game never uses the network); the test target builds for Mac, so all tests also run on My Mac.
- Kept in mind for eventual App Store release even though not submitting yet: Photos/camera usage description strings, no unnecessary permissions, standard content rating considerations.

### 16.1 Glossary & Bestiary **[Built]**

Recorded from the user (2026-10-18); Glossary and Bestiary built 2026-10-22, monster skills in the Bestiary the same day.

- **Discovery is meta-progression:** what's been discovered persists across runs (like card stats) in `discoveries.json` in Application Support (`DiscoveryStore`, via `UserDataLocation`; an unreadable file is backed up first; missing categories load as empty). It is a set of content ids per category — skills, potions, gear, relics, enemies — and only ever grows. Only real runs discover things; Test Fight does not.
- **What counts as encountered** (`RunState`):
  - skills: the heroes' known skills when a run starts or resumes, and every skill in a skill offer once the elite that carries it is won or the scroll is bought;
  - potions: all of them on entering a shop (every potion is on its shelf), plus potions won from fights or events;
  - gear and relics: the shop's rolled stock on entering it, fight rewards on victory, event finds;
  - enemies: every enemy of a fight when it starts (win or lose).
  - Runs resumed from before this existed also reveal the party's potions, relics and gear.
- **Glossary** (`GlossaryView`, menu → Glossary): a progress strip (skills / potions / gear / relics found out of all, a green seal when complete), then one parchment card per class's skills, then Potions, Gear and Relics, each with its found count. Every entry is a tile: found → its art (skill emblem / item art), name, cost in gold (items) and a symbol capsule (skill mana cost, cooldown and `SkillEffectSummary`; potion/gear/relic effect labels; gear slot icon) over a line of plain text (gear also names its slot and who can wear it). Not found → a dark silhouette of the art with a "?" and "???" / "Not found yet".
- **Bestiary** (`BestiaryView`, menu → Bestiary): a progress strip, then one card per monster (normal, then elite, then boss; content order within a tier). Met → sprite, tier badge, name, stats as symbols (♥ HP, basic attack dice with a violet sparkle when magic, hourglass = acts every N enemy turns, gold), then its moves (`EnemyMoveList`): the pattern as numbered rows — each skill's icon in its meaning color on a dark medallion, its name (violet sparkle when magic) and what it does in plain text ("2d6+2 magic damage to each hero, ignores block") — then, under "Rules — checked first, in order", one row per rule with the skill and its condition ("When below 50% HP · once per fight"). The fight's info sheet (`CharacterInfoSheet`) shows the same list with the move it will play next marked "Next". Not met → silhouette sprite, "???" and "Meet it in a fight to learn more."
- **Monster skills [Built 2026-10-22]:** enemies have their own skills and rules (§7.3); the Bestiary shows both once the monster has been met.
