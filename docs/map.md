# Study RPG — Map

Part of the game design reference; `game.md` is the index. Section numbers (§) are shared across all docs.

## 9. Map **[Built]**

Implemented by `MapGenerator` (pure function, `Services/MapGenerator.swift`) and `RunState` (`@Observable`, the whole run, `Services/RunState.swift`), `MapNode`/`GameMap` in `Models/RunModels.swift`, `RunMapView` in `Views/RunMapView.swift` and `RunHUD` in `Views/RunSymbols.swift` (modelled on the prototype's painted map, laid out horizontally). The `RunHUD` along the top shows: an abandon flag, each hero's portrait + HP bar + HP, level badge + XP bar (§5 "Levels") + mana, the training bonus (weightlifter + `+N`), then one bag button with the potions (dashed empty slots up to the bag limit), the gear-bag count, relics and gold, and a settings gear. Tapping a hero (in the HUD or the walking miniatures on the map) opens their page of `PartyInventorySheet`; the bag button opens its Bag page:
  - **Hero pages (one per hero)**, three columns: portrait + HP + mana + level, XP bar, `xp/next`, the hero's six die faces (§8 "Hero dice") + passive bonus chips (block, damage, first-roll dice, starting mana, healing — summed from relics, training and gear); the three **gear slots** (tap ↓ to take off) and the wearable gear from the bag (tap to wear); and the **skills** — every known skill with its symbols, cost and cooldown, equipped ones checked (tap to equip/unequip, `n/4` counter).
  - **Bag page**: gold, fights won, cards right/answered; potions (drink healing/mana potions on a chosen hero); the gear bag (tap a hero portrait to give it to them); relics.
  - During a fight, the fight's party button opens this same sheet, read-only, with the fight's live state (§7.6). Tapping a character's name on the battlefield still opens `CharacterInfoSheet` for that one character.

- **Tabletop parchment scroll** (`TabletopSurfaceView` + `WorldMapTerrain`, rasterized once into an image per map height, so scrolling costs nothing; the scroll pieces are in `Views/ParchmentMapArt.swift`): the horizontal map is an old parchment scroll lying open on a dark walnut/mahogany wooden tabletop (`TabletopSurfaceView` with wood planks, grain lines, plank seams, and warm candlelight vignette). The sheet doesn't fill the table top to bottom — about 9% of the height (16–60 pt) of wood shows above and below. Both ends are **rolled up** (`ScrollRollerView`: a shaded paper cylinder with a walnut knob and brass ring above and below), the top and bottom edges are **ragged with a few nicks** (`TornSheetShape`, fixed per act), and the paper is **worn**: water-stain rings, a scorch mark burnt into the top edge, shading where it curves into the rolls. It casts a dark offset shadow onto the wood and has a double-ruled cartographic ink border, a crimson wax seal, a river winding across the whole map, ink-sketched hills, a forest thick at the trailhead thinning out toward the end (drawn with the hand-drawn `map_fantasy_tree*` art), mountains around the boss and a few foothills (the `map_fantasy_mountain*` art), the dragon's ember glow behind the boss, and a compass rose at the trailhead. Scenery is kept clear of places and roads. Falls back to ink shapes if the art is missing.
- **Places** (`PaintedPlaceMark`, 60 pt, boss 84 pt): painted onto the parchment in ink and watercolor, not game icons — an uneven watercolor blot tinted per type under an ink drawing: crossed swords (fight), a skull (elite), a gilded crown (boss), a striped market tent with a pennant (shop), an inked italic question mark (event), crossed logs with a flame (campfire). Reachable ones get a gold outline (plus the gold pulse below), finished ones are ticked off with a red-ink check and lose most of their color, and places on paths no longer reachable fade out. Act II keeps its glass medallions (`WorldMapBadge`). Positions follow the generator's rows/lanes with a small deterministic jitter so it doesn't read as a grid (`WorldMapLayout`).
- **Roads** (`WorldMapRoads`, static and only redrawn when progress changes): winding S-curves from the trailhead and between places, painted on — a faint watercolor wash under an inked line: the route taken in red ink (like an old treasure map), the roads you can take next in ink dashes, dotted footpaths ahead, closed roads barely visible.
- **The walkable roads shine** (`OpenRoadShimmer`, user decision 2026-10-26 — this replaced "nothing on the map moves"): on the roads you can take next, gold dashes march toward each destination with a bright glint running along the road about every 1.7 s, and the places they lead to have a soft pulsing gold ring. It's its own small `TimelineView` layer (30 fps) above the static roads, so the map never redraws; it stops while a fight/shop/campfire/event covers the map (unless you're looking at it), while the party walks, when the run is over, and with Reduce Motion. Act II uses the same layer in cyan. Map art is drawn from small pre-scaled copies (`ArtCache`, §17 "Art sizing"), and the forest is sparse.
- **Walking party & transitions:** the two heroes (`WalkerFigureView` + `FollowRoad`, 62 pt tall) stand on the current place (or the trailhead) and can be tapped to inspect their character sheet. Tapping a place opens a preview card (`MapNodePreviewCard`, sized to its content): a header with the place's medallion, its name (Fight, Elite, Boss, Shop, Event, Campfire) and a 30pt close button; then for fights, the actual rolled enemies with their HP, attack dice and gold (+ a relic seal for elites); for a shop, its potions + relic; for a campfire, `+12 ♥ / +1 ↑`; for an event, its `EventSceneView` illustration. If it's reachable, the gold **Travel** button sends the party walking (otherwise a lock line "Not on your road") along the road to it (`0.9s` travel with animated alternating left/right stepping boots, bouncy step arcs, squash-and-stretch at footfalls, trailing dust puffs, the second hero a step behind, and a gold dotted trail behind them) while the map scrolls to follow. Fights, shops, campfires, events, and the end-of-run recap transition smoothly in-place via fast (`0.22s`) crossfades over the map so `RunMapView` stays alive underneath with zero rebuild delay.
- **Act II — the holographic map** (`mapStyle: hologram`, §16.0; `Views/HoloMapArt.swift`): the same layout, roads and places, drawn as a glowing holo-sheet over a dark console. `HoloConsoleSurfaceView` replaces the wooden table (brushed gunmetal, panel seams with status lights, a cyan underglow); `HoloMapTerrain` replaces the parchment painting (deep glass with a fine cyan grid and scanlines, a glowing data stream where the river was, circuit traces with solder pads, wireframe city blocks thinning toward the end, a red-and-cyan reactor glow with dashed rings and wireframe towers with red beacons around the boss, HUD corner brackets and an uplink glyph at the trailhead; `map_future_tower` / `map_future_block` art replaces the drawn props). Roads become lines of light (travelled: white core with a cyan halo; open: bright cyan dashes; ahead: faint cyan dashes), places are dark glass medallions with cyan rims and light glyph tints, and the sheet has a cyan edge and halo instead of paper layers. The terrain is rasterized once per height *and act*.
- The map's scenery is static (no flowing rivers or breathing walkers); the only motion is the walkable roads' shimmer, the party's walk when it travels, and scrolling.
- **Looking at the map (any time in a run):** every screen that covers the map — fight, shop, campfire, event, skill offer — has a round map chip (`MapPeekButton`; in the run HUD next to the heroes, in the fight's top bar and over its victory/defeat screen, top-right on the skill offer). It hides that screen (kept alive underneath, nothing is lost) and shows the map with a gold **Back** button at the bottom. While looking, places can be tapped for their preview but not travelled to. In a fight the chip only works while the fight waits for the player (choosing a skill/target, or the victory/defeat screen), so nothing plays out unseen.
- One map per run: a branching DAG of nodes from a start to a single boss node, laid out left→right. Node types: `fight`, `elite`, `boss`, `shop`, `event`, `campfire` (the existing `MapNodeType`). The start isn't a materialized node — the player can immediately pick any row-0 node.
- **Guarantees** (always true, regardless of randomness):
  - exactly one `boss` node, alone in the final row;
  - **the row right before the boss is all campfires** (`map.campfireBeforeBoss`, Slay-the-Spire style), and the row before that never rolls a campfire (no two rests in a row);
  - **no shop or campfire before `map.restMinProgress`** (25% → rows 0–1 are fights, events and nothing else);
  - **every route passes a shop or campfire before the campfire row**: while any route from a row-0 node to the last free row has neither, one of its fights/events (from `restMinProgress` on) becomes a shop or campfire;
  - at least one `elite` and one `shop`;
  - **never two shops in a row** (no road leads from a shop to a shop): the later one becomes a campfire, or a fight where a campfire isn't allowed, and the rest patch never places a shop next to a shop when a campfire fits;
  - **more fights than every other kind of place together** (fights > events + elites + shops + campfires + boss): events (keeping the map's last one), then extra elites, then shops/campfires that no route needs become fights — never the only elite or shop, the boss or the campfire row; only if that's still not enough does the last event go.
- **Size:** `balance.json → map`: `rowCount` 8 steps before the boss (so a run is 8 places + the boss), `maxLanes` 3 parallel roads at most, `pathCount` 3 walks, `minLanes` 2 → about 17–22 places per map (avg ~18). This was cut down from 13 rows × 4 lanes × 6 walks (~40 places), which made runs too long and the map too convoluted.
- **Algorithm:** `map.pathCount` independent random walks run from row 0 to the boss row, each shifting lane by at most ±1 per row; every walk's nodes and edges are unioned into one graph. **Roads never cross:** a walk only takes a lane shift whose road wouldn't cross an existing road, falling back to going straight. This gives every node reachability from the start and to the boss for free. Rows short of `map.minLanes` get padded with extra nodes, but only where they can be wired to the neighbouring rows without crossing. Node types are rolled from `map.nodeWeights` (fight 0.55, event 0.2, shop 0.08, campfire 0.07, elite 0.15 — elites only from `map.eliteMinProgress` (33%) of the way in, shops/campfires only from `map.restMinProgress`). Then the guarantees above are patched in if missing; a patch never overwrites the only node of another guaranteed type, the boss or the campfire row. Every guarantee is checked on 400 seeded maps by `MapGeneratorTests`.
- **Reachability at any moment**: only the current node's `nextNodeIDs` (or, before the first pick, any row-0 node) are selectable; everything else is visible but locked.
- Each row maps to one of `encounters.json`'s three depth tiers by thirds (`MapGenerator.depthTier(forRow:rowCount:)`), so difficulty still scales with how far along the map a node is, same as before.
- **Node handling:**
  - fight/elite/boss → the fight is built once on tap (encounter and reward rolled once per node and saved) and pushed; after a won fight pending die upgrades (§5 "Levels") and then a pending skill offer are shown first;
  - shop/campfire/event → open full screen: their scenery and the run HUD's strip reach the screen edges (under the notch), while wares, buttons and cards stay inside the safe area;
  - every node is marked visited and becomes the current node when finished.
